An evidence-informed planning model

How we estimate the value of reducing workplace back strain.

Backy provides real-time haptic feedback when workers reach configured high-risk postures. Reduced exposure may lead to fewer back-related injuries and less absence over time. This calculator estimates the resulting potential financial value; it does not predict or guarantee fewer claims.

Current Backy public ROI Explorer (November 2026)

The public Backy ROI Explorer has three inputs (industry, selected market, equipped workforce) and a 1-year, 3-year and 5-year period selector. Only APAC, United States, Canada, United Kingdom, Germany and the Netherlands appear; Australia is included in APAC and Singapore is not a separate public choice. APAC uses Australian risk data and AUD planning costs rather than an established region-wide injury-cost average.

Backy's reported logistics pilot saw a 67% reduction in high-risk posture events per day; hospitality and healthcare pilots reported 29.5% and 21.4% reductions in time spent in risky postures, respectively. Construction, manufacturing, cleaning and retail values are illustrative pilot targets, not established minimum Backy outcomes. These measures are not direct evidence of reduced back injury incidence.

The Australian industry frequency benchmarks come from Safe Work Australia's Key Work Health and Safety Statistics 2026, reflecting all serious injury types, not back injuries alone. The national 35.2% body-stressing share is a coarse proxy for potentially relevant injuries, and the 40% posture-to-injury conversion is a hypothetical planning coefficient, not scientifically established.

Current model: 1, 3 or 5 years

Expected annual serious claims = equipped employees × 2,000 assumed hours per year × Australian industry serious claim frequency per million hours ÷ 1,000,000 Annual potentially relevant injury proxy = expected annual claims × 35.2% body-stressing share Hypothetical injury-frequency reduction = reported or assumed postural change × fixed 40% conversion Expected injury cases affected over period = annual proxy × hypothetical injury reduction × years Potential employer operating-cost saving = cases affected × (Australian industry median lost weeks × 5 workdays × assumed local lost-day cost + 500 local currency administration cost) Backy full investment = equipped employees × (one-time device price + 12 × years × monthly software cost) ROI = (potential employer cost savings − full Backy investment) ÷ full investment × 100%

A 1-year analysis includes 12 subscription months, 3-year analysis 36 and 5-year analysis 60. The one-time device price is counted only once in all three scenarios. Daily operating loss, fixed administrative cost, device pricing, currency exchange rates, annual hours and the 40% conversion are fixed scenario assumptions; market-specific averages and validated causal effects are not established by these inputs.

The sign of the result must remain accurate: a calculated loss produces negative ROI. The customer-facing explanation presents modeled savings compared with the full investment, together with the annual employer-loss baseline needed for payback when net ROI is negative. In this particular linear pricing and risk-rate model, employee count scales savings and costs proportionally and thus does not itself change ROI%; meaningful company-level positive ROI requires different validated baseline exposure, avoided operating costs, accepted pricing or observed impact.

Historical comparison: The separate original multi-market Backy calculator used a broader injury and non-claim absence structure, including optional employer-retained compensation. Its defaults do not universally yield a positive ROI either. The present public calculation does not count insurer-paid statutory compensation as a direct cash saving to the employer.

Three steps to a credible ROI estimate

1 · Establish the baseline

Use historical lost-time cases, days absent, role-specific injury risk and actual employer costs, or the clearly labeled national proxies.

2 · Test injury-reduction scenarios

Apply conservative, expected and optimistic assumptions. A 20% fall in high-risk postures is not the same as 20% fewer injuries.

3 · Subtract the Backy investment

Model one-time device charges plus dashboard fees across one or three years, then compare cost with the avoidable financial burden.

The exact formulas

Compensated injury cases

Expected cases prevented = Workers × (relevant cases per 1,000 ÷ 1,000) × sector multiplier × assumed reduction × years

The model takes a country-specific annual injury baseline and multiplies it by the selected workforce exposure and sector. Sector multipliers of 1.0–2.2 are illustrative planning inputs, not official published sector rates.

Financial loss per relevant case

Avoidable case cost = days lost × avoidable daily employer cost + claim amount × employer-retained share + additional admin cost

Claims paid entirely by an insurer must not be claimed as direct savings to the employer. Premium reductions count only if supported by actual experience-rating or insurer terms, and must not be counted twice.

Other back-related absence

Absence savings = workers × other back-absence days per worker/year × sector multiplier × assumed reduction × years × (daily cost + treatment cost ÷ 5 days)

The five-day duration is an assumed episode length to allocate treatment costs. The other-absence stream excludes the working days already counted in the claims stream. The UK, Germany and the Netherlands use absence as the principal proxy in this simplified model; that does not exclude other liabilities in those countries.

Return on investment

Gross savings = claim savings + other absence savings
Investment = workers × (applicable one-time device price + monthly dashboard fee × 12 × years)
Net savings = gross savings − investment
ROI (%) = net savings ÷ investment × 100

Break-even injury reduction is the reduction needed to make estimated gross savings equal the Backy investment. A threshold above 100% means this particular model cannot pay back through the modeled injury and absence savings alone.

Important distinction: The 10%, 20% and 30% reductions are planning scenarios, not clinically or actuarially validated Backy outcomes. The U.S. Government Accountability Office found limited evidence that occupational wearables reduce injuries. Review the GAO assessment ↗

Backy pilot measurements and fixed customer-facing settings

The public industry comparison uses a locked, company-reported logistics reduction of 67% fewer HRP events per day (9.8 to 3.3), a hospitality reduction of 29.5% less time in risky postures, and a hospital result of 21.4% less time in risky postures. The hospital report additionally states 14.5% fewer high-risk events and 34% fewer forward bends. These are different measures, not interchangeable evidence of fewer injuries.

The source is TacnIQ.ai's Backy for Safety Teams presentation, pilot outcomes reported with vibration feedback active and customer identities anonymised. The remaining construction, manufacturing, cleaning/facilities and retail percentages are hypothetical planning values, not Backy trial results.

Locked injury conversion assumption

Illustrative injury reduction = measured or assumed posture improvement × 40% assumed transfer coefficient

The transfer coefficient is locked in the public dashboard so visitors cannot alter the model's reported risk percentages. It is deliberately not presented as empirically validated by Backy: different measures (events versus time exposed), task-specific exposures and multiple causes of injury prevent a directly validated one-to-one causal mapping from currently available data. The true coefficient could be higher, lower or zero.

Documented Singapore back injury financial cases

Case A — work injury compensation S$26,200

Durairaj Santiran v Singapore Airlines Ltd [2024] SGHC 249, paragraph 9, records a S$26,200 WICA award for an earlier workplace back injury sustained while handling an overhead luggage compartment; treatment included painkillers and physiotherapy. This is an actual historical award for one worker, not a representative mean or projected employer cash saving.

Case B — civil damages S$15,000 for lumbar injury

[2004] SGHC 100 concerned an industrial worker injured lifting and stacking connector forgings. The court confirmed an S$15,000 pain-and-suffering component for lumbar disc injury; other earning-capacity and medical components were assessed separately. Historical civil damages are not automatically comparable to WICA compensation or employer-retained claims.

Independent wearable case study — not a Backy conversion rate

StrongArm / PetSmart, December 2024 update reported 8% fewer high-risk bends, 13% lower StrongArm Preventable Injury rates, and 41% lower incurred costs. These relate to a different wearable, workforce and study periods, and cannot justify applying a universal 13/8 or 41/8 multiplier to Backy's 67% result. GAO independently observed limited evidence that ergonomic sensors reduce actual workplace injuries.

Insurance treatment: For the Singapore court example, the customer dashboard offers an optional S$26,200 single-case severity sensitivity only. Insurer-paid compensation remains on the insurer side of the calculation; employer-retained losses, replacement labor, noninsured treatment and independently confirmed future premium movement form the employer's financial case. No national average can be inferred from a court judgment.

Employer-funded Backy: industry benchmarking and evidence grades

Employer ROI is based on employer-paid absences, business interruption, incident management, the employer-retained share of workers' compensation, employer-funded non-overlapping physiotherapy and only a separately confirmed premium change. The full payout by an insurer is not a direct employer saving.

Further documented employer cost drivers and evidence for the Backy business case

Current public presentation: The public Backy Impact Explorer presents only an estimated signed three-year ROI, possible operating costs avoided, the estimated three-year Backy investment, and the posture-improvement measure, with a short, dynamically calculated explanation of how much of the deployment cost is offset by potential avoided operating costs. ROI is calculated as (operating cost avoidance − Backy spend) / Backy spend × 100%. A negative ROI indicates modeled savings are insufficient to cover spend. The page must not claim the projected offset is a percentage reduction in the employer's total operating expenditure, since that total is unknown.

Strong sources for future commercial materials

US insurance and manual handling: Liberty Mutual Workplace Safety Index 2025 estimates US$58.78 billion per year in costs for disabling workplace injuries, of which the top ten causes account for US$50.87 billion. Overexertion involving outside sources (especially material handling) accounts for approximately US$13.7 billion. The Index reflects 2022 underlying injury data and mostly workers' compensation costs, not money an individual employer will save by purchasing Backy.

UK 2025/26 health burden: UK HSE's 19 November 2026 statistical release reports 477,000 workers with work-related musculoskeletal disorders and an estimated total 39.6 million working days lost due to work-related ill health and workplace injury. Its work-related musculoskeletal disorders category accounted for about 7.5 million working days lost according to the HSE working-days-lost statistics. These are national burdens, not Backy-preventable case counts or employer-specific absence.

US operating cost categories: OSHA Safety Pays includes replacement-worker hiring/training, injury investigation and administration, lost and disrupted productivity and wages paid for non-productive time as indirect employer expenses. They are plausible customer-specific ROI categories only if they are genuinely avoidable and do not duplicate the value of absent workdays already entered.

Injury evidence limitations: The US GAO's 2025 wearable ergonomics assessment states that ergonomic sensors can improve safety-related measures but currently have limited public causal evidence of injury reduction. Backy's 67%, 29.5% and 21.4% results are posture-event and exposure-time measures. The 40% posture-to-claims transfer coefficient is an assumption, not a scientific estimate.

Earlier studies to follow up: Dunning and colleagues, 2010, analyzed musculoskeletal workers' compensation claims across industries and observed high cost per claim for lumbar, shoulder and cervical spine injuries; see PubMed. Bhattacharya (2014) explored occupational MSD costs and their direct/indirect components; see full text. These historic figures should not replace 2026 cost benchmarks without adjusting for study age and insurance structure.

Audit preparation or reassessment efficiency — prospective, not yet priced

A 2025 sensor-based ergonomic risk assessment study describes how automating data capture and analysis reduces repeated manual video reviews and associated assessment time, but does not provide a universal monetary saving for Backy. OSHA recommends recurring workplace inspections and risk assessments; Singapore MOM directs companies to review risk assessments at least once every three years, and in certain event-triggered situations. Backy should be positioned as supporting evidence collection and more targeted follow-up, not replacing required audits.

Qualified customer-specific method, when supported: (manual ergonomic review and report hours genuinely eliminated each year × fully loaded hourly safety-professional cost + avoided external consultant invoices) × forecast years. Subtract new Backy data review time, and do not double-count operational staff time already included in other loss categories. Measure time before and after deployment at an actual site. Until validated, keep savings at zero in the public ROI.

Regulatory fines: Do not attribute automatic savings to detected poor postures or warnings. OSHA's ergonomics enforcement guidance says certain serious recognized ergonomic hazards can be cited under the general duty clause, but a single risky posture does not create a predictable citation. Expected-fine savings are unsuitable for the public calculator; regulatory risk management can be a qualitative benefit.

Before using a result in a customer proposal

  • Replace planning estimates with three years of the employer's actual back-related cases, absence, claim payments, treatment and replacement staffing costs.
  • Separate company-paid costs from insurer-paid compensation; verify any premium reductions with the broker or insurer.
  • Enter the correct contractual hardware and dashboard pricing. The default prices in the source calculator are examples that require confirmation.
  • Verify whether injuries and other back-related absence days overlap. Never count the same days or paid amounts twice.
  • Compare companies using consistent injury definitions. National studies are not directly comparable.
  • The three-year model assumes a constant workforce, impact and pricing, without inflation or discounting. Exchange rates are approximate, not live.
  • Use measured high-risk posture reductions from Backy pilots as behavioral evidence, not a proven claim reduction.
