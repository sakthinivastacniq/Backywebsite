An evidence-informed planning model

How we estimate the value of reducing workplace back strain.

Backy provides real-time haptic feedback when workers reach configured high-risk postures. Reduced exposure may lead to fewer back-related injuries and less absence over time. This calculator estimates the resulting potential financial value; it does not predict or guarantee fewer claims.

Three steps to a credible ROI estimate

1 · Establish the baseline

Use historical lost-time cases, days absent, role-specific injury risk and actual employer costs, or the clearly labeled national proxies.

2 · Test injury-reduction scenarios

Apply conservative, expected and optimistic assumptions. A 20% fall in high-risk postures is not the same as 20% fewer injuries.

3 · Subtract the Backy investment

Model one-time device charges plus dashboard fees across one or three years, then compare cost with the avoidable financial burden.

The exact formulas

Compensated injury cases

Expected cases prevented = Workers × (relevant cases per 1,000 ÷ 1,000) × sector multiplier × assumed reduction × years

The model takes a country-specific annual injury baseline and multiplies it by the selected workforce exposure and sector. Sector multipliers of 1.0–2.2 are illustrative planning inputs, not official published sector rates.

Financial loss per relevant case

Avoidable case cost = days lost × avoidable daily employer cost + claim amount × employer-retained share + additional admin cost

Claims paid entirely by an insurer must not be claimed as direct savings to the employer. Premium reductions count only if supported by actual experience-rating or insurer terms, and must not be counted twice.

Other back-related absence

Absence savings = workers × other back-absence days per worker/year × sector multiplier × assumed reduction × years × (daily cost + treatment cost ÷ 5 days)

The five-day duration is an assumed episode length to allocate treatment costs. The other-absence stream excludes the working days already counted in the claims stream. The UK, Germany and the Netherlands use absence as the principal proxy in this simplified model; that does not exclude other liabilities in those countries.

Return on investment

Gross savings = claim savings + other absence savings
Investment = workers × (applicable one-time device price + monthly dashboard fee × 12 × years)
Net savings = gross savings − investment
ROI (%) = net savings ÷ investment × 100

Break-even injury reduction is the reduction needed to make estimated gross savings equal the Backy investment. A threshold above 100% means this particular model cannot pay back through the modeled injury and absence savings alone.

Important distinction: The 10%, 20% and 30% reductions are planning scenarios, not clinically or actuarially validated Backy outcomes. The U.S. Government Accountability Office found limited evidence that occupational wearables reduce injuries. Review the GAO assessment ↗

Before using a result in a customer proposal

  • Replace planning estimates with three years of the employer's actual back-related cases, absence, claim payments, treatment and replacement staffing costs.
  • Separate company-paid costs from insurer-paid compensation; verify any premium reductions with the broker or insurer.
  • Enter the correct contractual hardware and dashboard pricing. The default prices in the source calculator are examples that require confirmation.
  • Verify whether injuries and other back-related absence days overlap. Never count the same days or paid amounts twice.
  • Compare companies using consistent injury definitions. National studies are not directly comparable.
  • The three-year model assumes a constant workforce, impact and pricing, without inflation or discounting. Exchange rates are approximate, not live.
  • Use measured high-risk posture reductions from Backy pilots as behavioral evidence, not a proven claim reduction.
