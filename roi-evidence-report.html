01 / Executive summary

Back pain can affect a business even without a compensable injury: employees may miss shifts, require cover or work below capacity. Backy uses real-time posture detection and haptic feedback to coach safer movement. The business case is the potential reduction in these losses, measured against equipment and software spend.

US$13.7bn

Low back pain is the leading global cause of disability; the GBD study attributes 38.8% of low-back-pain disability to occupational ergonomics, smoking and high BMI combined. The global 619 million estimate is not a count of occupational cases. [1] [2] The financial model below focuses on high-exposure roles, rather than attributing this whole disease burden to Backy.

Evidence hierarchy: government and insurer data establish exposure and cost benchmarks; peer-reviewed research establishes potential mechanisms and productivity burden; company-reported pilots measure posture changes; scenario assumptions translate these into possible economic outcomes. The last step is not yet independently validated for Backy.

03 / The cost-of-risk pillars

A. Injury exposure and claim severity

Australia recorded approximately 147,200 serious claims in 2024–25 provisional data, including 51,800 body-stressing claims (35.2%). The median body-stressing claim involved 9.5 weeks away and A$21,200 in compensation. [3] The claim frequency and median time lost inform the model; the compensation amount is context only. A median is not a mean expected cost.

US National Safety Council estimated US$181.4 billion of total work-injury economic costs in 2024, including US$15.5 billion of uninsured employer costs. Its NCCI-based claim-cost statistics report US$50,076 per claim on average for 2023–24 accidents. [5] [6] These are all-injury figures, not lumbar-only claim costs; neither is added to projected savings. HSE reports 477,000 workers with work-related MSDs and 39.6 million days lost to all work-related ill health and nonfatal injury in 2025/26. [8] [9]

B. Absenteeism and replacement staffing

In a 2019 study of nearly 2,000 US manual material handlers, 25% reported back pain lasting at least seven days in the prior year, 14% sought care and 10% reported lost time. These are overlapping outcomes, not additive. [12] The calculator estimates serious absence first, then subtracts it from a broader excess-absence proxy before adding any positive residual. It allocates each recovered hour to either uncovered production capacity or covered replacement labour, not both.

OSHA's Safety Pays tool uses indirect/direct cost factors ranging from 4.5 for claims below US$3,000 to 1.1 for claims of US$10,000 or more, based on limited historical construction evidence. [11] This demonstrates why indirect costs matter; the multipliers are not applied on top of the calculator's itemised components.

C. Presenteeism and productive time

Presenteeism describes impaired performance while present at work. A 2025 population model draws on a 2008 US survey and uses these rates: [13] [14]

Earlier US survey research also found that most time lost to common pain conditions came from reduced performance at work rather than absence. [15] These findings motivate the model's separate at-work time pool. They do not establish the proportion of losses recoverable through Backy. The population study's A$638.3bn projected decade-long GDP loss includes workforce participation impacts and is not a direct employer expense. [13]

D. Insurance

Premiums and retained claim costs depend on local arrangements. The model uses regional premium references and a hypothetical fraction of targeted risk improvement reflected in renewal prices from year two onward. It excludes insurer-paid claims and does not promise a discount. See the next section for market inputs.

E. Assessment efficiency

A 2025 sensor-based ergonomic assessment workflow using inertial sensors and muscle measurements reported a reduction in analysis time approaching half. [24] That result is not a Backy assessment study. The calculator assumes a modest analyst workload and a hypothetical reduction in review time; it does not imply that Backy replaces statutory assessments or guarantees external fee savings.

05 / What wearable evidence does and does not establish

Real-time feedback has a plausible role in reducing risky trunk movements during tasks. Research on 15 warehouse workers examined vibrotactile posture-correction training in manual sorting, and a 2023 simulated construction study reported improved trunk-flexion measures during selected tasks. [25] [26] However, a 2026 distribution-centre field study reported no initial improvement during early feedback days. [28] These findings reinforce the importance of task fit, uptake and longitudinal measurement.

Backy's company-reported pilot metrics are 67% fewer high-risk posture events in logistics, 29.5% less time in risky postures in hospitality and 21.4% less time in risky postures in healthcare. The other four sectors use illustrative pilot targets. Event counts and exposure duration measure different behaviours; neither directly measures injury frequency, claims severity, productivity or cash savings. These figures are reproduced from the current Backy calculator, not independently audited in this review.

The GAO's 2025 assessment found limited public evidence that occupational wearables reduce worker injuries, including limitations from short deployment studies. [27] Accordingly, this evidence review does not claim clinical proof of Backy injury prevention, guaranteed full-shift detection, or validated financial efficacy.

Step 1 — estimate time and loss exposure

Let N be equipped workers, Y years, H scheduled hours, F occupational serious claims per million hours, S body-stressing share, L gross hourly wage, and P the posture improvement fraction.

H = N × 2,000 × Y; L = local annual gross wage ÷ 2,000
Serious absence A = min(H, H × F ÷ 1,000,000 × S × 9.5 × 5 × 8)
Pain cohort C = H × 15%; excess absence B = C × (6.96% − 4.03%)
Residual absence R = max(0, B − A); at-work cohort W = max(0, C − max(C × 6.96%, A + R))
Excess at-work productivity hours E = W × (27.6% − 14.3%)

Step 2 — estimate recoverable value

Hypothetical injury reduction Q = P × 40%; recovered absence T = A × Q + R × 10%
Uncovered absence benefit = T × 50% × L
Replacement benefit = T × 50% × L × 1.25
Presenteeism benefit = E × L × 10%
Insurance benefit = annual reference premium × max(0, Y − 1) × S × Q × 25%
Assessment benefit = N ÷ 10 × 4 × Y × L × 25%

Step 3 — compare with programme spend

Combined benefit = sum of the five benefit components
Investment = N × (volume-tier device price + monthly subscription × 12 × Y)
Net benefit = combined benefit − investment; net ROI (%) = net benefit ÷ investment × 100

Break-even interpretation

When the forecast is below break-even, the calculator converts the remaining annual shortfall into equivalent additional productive time per employee per shift at the reference gross wage. It is a conditional target, not observed productivity recovery.

Additional seconds per shift = max(0, investment − benefit) ÷ Y ÷ (N × annual wage) × 2,000 ÷ 250 × 3,600

08 / Enterprise validation and next steps

Before treating the scenario as a customer investment case, replace proxy inputs with the employer's own historic back-related incidents, medically certified absence, overtime and staffing invoices, wages, insurance terms and actual assessment workload. Confirm device utilisation and wear time.

Use a baseline and matched comparison where possible, with equivalent task mix and throughput/quality measures. Assess posture events per wear-hour, exposure duration, pain and work-function outcomes, actual replacement spending, assessment time net of dashboard review, and verified renewal terms. Longer follow-up is needed for injury frequency and compensation costs; a short pilot cannot establish long-term claims savings. Report missing data, adverse results and uncertainty alongside positive changes.

Scope of this report: a narrative desk review and transparent planning model, not a systematic review, peer-reviewed publication, insurer-endorsed forecast or controlled Backy clinical trial. The draft's unsupported universal detection accuracy, 16.7-minute productivity figure, clinical-proof claims and guaranteed payback/bid exclusions have not been used as factual inputs. Some regional benchmarks are state/province references; occupational rates remain shared across markets. The report supports the rationale for investigation, not proof of every assumption.

10 / Global evidence and higher-exposure sensitivity

Evidence from Asia and Africa strengthens the need for ergonomic risk management, while the financial comparison remains an equal-weighted benchmark of the original six reference markets. Japan, Singapore, South Korea and Sweden have been added as individual scenarios, without changing the original comparison series.

395 million

Estimated workers sustaining nonfatal occupational injuries worldwide, across all injury types. ILO, 2023.

23.3%

Back-pain prevalence in a cross-sectional study of employees at a large Japanese company. Industrial Health, 2025.

59.0%

Pooled back-pain prevalence in the southern African subgroup of a review of nurses, not all workers. BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders, 2020.

Low back pain was also among the leading reported causes of presenteeism in a 2023 survey of 10,000 Japanese workers. Yoshimoto et al., 2025. These prevalence and survey findings use different definitions and populations; they do not establish a persistent-pain rate for all industries, a financial loss coefficient, or Backy efficacy.

Higher-exposure workforce scenario

For a site with confirmed long-term back pain in 25% of equipped employees, the calculator replaces the standard 15% cohort proxy with 25% in the cohort-hours equation C = H × pain prevalence. All injury-risk, recovery, replacement, insurance, assessment and investment assumptions remain unchanged. The 25% figure is a conditional sensitivity parameter, not a prevalence estimate derived from the above studies. The same overlap controls apply. No extra claim payments, productivity multipliers or guaranteed discounts are introduced.
